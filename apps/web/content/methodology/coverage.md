GTMI does not target 100% indicator coverage as a quality metric. Some
indicators we defined do not correspond to data the country actually
publishes — for example, several jurisdictions do not publish admission
statistics in structured form, so E.1.2 (cumulative approvals) stays
empty for them. The credibility design is to publish only what we can
defensibly source, surface what is missing per programme on the same
page as the score, and let the reader apply their own credibility
weighting via the &ldquo;Insufficient disclosure&rdquo; flag.

The realistic per-programme ceiling is around 28&ndash;31 indicators of
33 once the full cohort is scored. Programmes below 70% coverage on any
pillar are flagged &ldquo;insufficient disclosure&rdquo; and withheld
from the public ranking entirely.

Three handling regimes for the unfilled indicators:

1. **Genuine country-level transparency gaps** &mdash; the country does
   not publish the underlying data. The indicator stays null; the
   sub-factor missing-data penalty applies; if pillar coverage drops
   below 70%, the programme is flagged.
2. **Indicators that do not correspond to publishable data anywhere**
   &mdash; methodology-version review decides per indicator whether to
   drop, restructure, or substitute country-level data. Existing scores
   keep their version stamp.
3. **Indicators where Tier&nbsp;1 is silent but Tier&nbsp;2 covers the
   gap** &mdash; an explicit allowlist authorises Tier&nbsp;2 backfill
   for specific indicators only, with the source tier visible in the
   provenance display so the reader sees the credibility tradeoff.
