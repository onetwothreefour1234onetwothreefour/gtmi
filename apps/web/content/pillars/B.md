Process Design measures how hard the application itself is — the friction
the candidate experiences between deciding to apply and receiving a
decision. At 15% of PAQ it is the lowest-weighted pillar, deliberately,
because process friction is recoverable: a slow programme can become
fast, a paper form can be digitised. The pillar still matters because
process quality predicts whether a candidate completes the application
at all.

Four sub-factors. Speed (30% of pillar) covers the published service-
level processing time and whether a fast track is offered. Complexity
(20%) captures the number of mandatory application steps and required
in-person touchpoints. Cost (30%) is a single all-in USD figure for a
principal applicant plus one spouse and two children, extracted directly
with inline currency conversion. Transparency (20%) covers the clarity
of the appeal-and-refusal process and whether applicants can self-serve
a status check. High scorers publish a binding SLA, run a digital-first
pipeline, and minimise the out-of-pocket cost the applicant carries
personally.
