Benefits measures what the visa holder can actually do once granted. At
20% of PAQ it sits in the middle of the pillar weight ranking — important
because the practical experience of holding the visa is what determines
whether candidates accept an offer and whether they stay long enough to
contribute meaningfully. The pillar was renamed from &ldquo;Rights&rdquo;
in methodology v4.0.0 (ADR-030); &ldquo;Benefits&rdquo; better matches
the applicant-facing framing of what the visa actually delivers.

Three sub-factors. Work Flexibility (40% of pillar) covers freedom to
switch employers, freedom to take on self-employment or secondary income,
and the visa&rsquo;s duration and renewability. Family (40%) captures
whether spouses are automatically included with work rights, the
dependent child age cap, and whether extended family inclusion is
available. Social Access (20%) records whether the visa holder and
their children can use public healthcare and public education on the
same terms as residents. High scorers minimise tying the visa to a
single employer, automatically include the immediate family with work
rights, and grant access to public services on day one rather than
after a qualifying period.
